# Concrete aftermath

The owner's new direction is believable, low-resolution, severely weathered
reinforced concrete. This study has new geometry, material formulas and lighting.
It uses the existing renderer and game infrastructure, but bypasses the old
porcelain/wear material pipeline.

## Material reasoning

Start with a cast cement body around a buried steel cage. Casting marks survive
on the original face; coarse angular aggregate becomes prominent where cover is
lost. Corrosion opens cracks and removes cover around reinforcement. Water leaves
vertical runoff, pale leachate and a darker foot. Fire deposits soot and changes
the binder; blast damage removes the upper section and bends exposed bars.

These are authored procedural interpretations, not a structural, chemical or
nuclear-blast simulation. The physical reference points are the
[FHWA concrete distress guide](https://www.fhwa.dot.gov/publications/research/infrastructure/pavements/pccp/01163/app.cfm)
and [NIST's investigation of fire-affected concrete](https://www.nist.gov/programs-projects/investigation-fire-affected-concretes-residual-properties-and-link-petrographic).

| Wall location | History | Main evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Rear, z=3 | Sound casting | Form ties, lift lines, restrained aggregate |
| Long middle wall, z=6 | Blast | Lost crown, angular spalls, bent cage ends, soot |
| Left, z=10 | Rain | Runoff, leaching, damp base |
| Front, z=11 | Corrosion | Cracks, lost cover, exposed oxidized steel |
| Right, x=13 | Fire | Soot, heat discoloration, fractured cover |

## Implementation

- `crates/rt-viewer/src/concrete.rs`: deterministic displaced wall meshes,
  polygonal fracture fronts, independent steel cages and ground-contact rubble.
  Vertex UVs carry actual cover loss and crack coverage into the shader.
- `crates/rt-probe/src/shaders/concrete.inc`: one source compiled into both GLSL
  and Metal. Cement, angular aggregate, pores, casting, water, oxide and heat
  have separate responses. Detail coverage follows the pixel footprint.
- `Look::concrete` and the `CONCRETE` material flag select this family explicitly.
  Material alpha encodes component/history; it is not the old wear word.
- The `aftermath` look uses subdued mineral colors, dusty paving and neutral
  daylight. The new `concrete aftermath` demo provides the five comparisons.

Geometry participates in shadows and baked GI. Fine procedural albedo is evaluated
at the camera hit; the GI bake uses average material colors. Existing player
occlusion dithering remains visible when a wall blocks the character. The legacy
wear editor does not author these new wall profiles; their parameters live in
`concrete.rs`. No additional ray queries were added by the material shader.

## Verified on Apple M2 Pro / Metal

- `cargo test --workspace`: 198 passed; release build and `git diff --check` passed.
- Three concrete tests cover all authored histories and legacy-pipeline bypass,
  exposed cage with retained rear concrete, and deterministic finite meshes.
- Actual `boot_demo` transitions: concrete → courtyard → concrete → effect
  catalogue → concrete, including a same-level rebuild; no crash.
- Scene: 42 primitives, 176,798 triangles, 12,870 GI probes. GI bake about 1.6 s.
- 180-frame headless benchmark at 2560×1600, `PIXEL=4`: 21.01 ms average CPU
  frame time. This is a harness measurement, not a guaranteed windowed frame rate.
- 480-frame/8-second movement recording completed. RGB differences between
  stationary frames 20/50 and 440/470 are exactly zero. Inspected start, movement,
  end, overview and enlarged blast detail.

Artifacts: `output/concrete/overview.png`, `blast-detail.png`, `walkthrough.mp4`,
and the reproducible `walk.trace` plus individual frames.

Launch on Retina:

```sh
LEVEL='concrete aftermath' WINDOW=1280x800 PIXEL=4 ./target/release/viewer
```
